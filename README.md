# 05 Into the Wind

**▶ Play it:** https://hanji-xu.github.io/into-the-wind/

Why animals turn their heads into the wind. A furry head seen from above carries about 1600 guard hairs. Each hair is an elastic keratin rod on a root spring, leaning back toward the tail. Air flows around the head (potential flow over a sphere, with a separated wake past 105°), slows inside the coat (exponential canopy profile), and pushes on each hair with cylinder drag, C_D = 1 + 10·Re^(−2/3).

Wind from the front runs with the grain, so drag presses the coat flat. Wind from behind runs against it, lifts the hair tips into faster air, and they flip forward across the face and eyes. A polar plot shows the steady-state share of ruffled hairs for every wind direction.

- Drag outside the head to move the wind; drag the head to turn it.
- "Let it turn on its own": the animal only feels how disturbed its fur is and does a run-and-tumble search. It ends up nose into the wind, then fidgets within about 45° of it. This behaviour rule is illustrative, not physiology.
- Five coats: red fox, house cat, husky, bison, long-haired dog. Sliders for hair length, diameter, keratin modulus, lie angle and coat shelter; wind speed, turbulence and slow motion.
- Everything runs at true scale, with no magnification. Equations and constants are listed in the page, and estimated values are marked ★.
